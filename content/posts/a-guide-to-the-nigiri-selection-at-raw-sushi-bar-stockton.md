<p>Nigiri is served two pieces per order at Raw Sushi Bar, which makes it easy to mix and match a few different types in one meal. Here's what's actually on the list.</p>
<h2>Familiar Starting Points</h2>
<p>Maguro (tuna), Ebi (shrimp), and Hamachi (yellowtail) are a solid entry point if you're newer to nigiri — clean, straightforward flavors without much preparation beyond the fish itself. Fresh Sake and Smoked Sake (both salmon preparations) are similarly approachable.</p>
<h2>For the More Adventurous</h2>
<p>Uni (sea urchin) is seasonal and priced at market, along with a version served with quail egg. Ikura (salmon roe) and Ikura with quail egg are good options if you want to try something with more texture. Tako (octopus) and Ika (squid) round out the less common picks.</p>
<h2>Cooked and Prepared Options</h2>
<p>Unagi (BBQ eel) is served warm with sauce, and Inari (tofu pouch) is a vegetarian nigiri option — both good choices if raw fish isn't quite what you're after that day.</p>
<h2>A Couple of House Specialties</h2>
<p>Spicy Sesame Seared Tuna and Spicy Tempura Scallop (served four pieces) both bring a cooked or seared element with some heat, which makes them a good middle ground between fully raw and fully cooked nigiri.</p>
<h2>How to Order</h2>
<p>Since nigiri comes two pieces per order, it's easy to order three or four different types across the table and share. Ama Ebi (sweet shrimp), Hotate (giant scallop), and Shiro Maguro (albacore) round out the rest of the list if you want to keep exploring.</p>
