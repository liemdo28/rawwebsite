<p>Central Valley summers regularly push into the 90s and above, and Stockton is no exception. When the last thing you want to do is turn on an oven or stand over a stove, a cold meal solves a real problem.</p>
<h2>Why Cold Food Makes Sense in the Heat</h2>
<p>Sashimi, nigiri, and most rolls are served cold or at room temperature, so there's no extra heat added to your kitchen or your evening. It's a practical option for a hot Tuesday as much as a special occasion.</p>
<h2>What to Order When It's Hot Out</h2>
<p>Sashimi platters (like the Sashimi Sunomono or Assorted Sashimi, nine pieces of tuna, salmon, and yellowtail) are fully cold. Cucumber-forward rolls and salads like the Wakame Salad or Green Salad add something light alongside them. If you want a little more substance without heat, cold nigiri covers that too.</p>
<h2>Takeout Keeps the Kitchen Cool</h2>
<p>Ordering ahead for pickup or delivery means you can skip cooking entirely on a hot evening. Sushi travels well over short distances since it's meant to be served cold, unlike a lot of hot takeout that loses quality on the way home.</p>
<h2>A Simple Seasonal Habit</h2>
<p>A lot of regulars treat sushi as a warm-weather default rather than a special-occasion meal — it's fast, it's cold, and it doesn't add to a hot house. If you're looking for a dinner plan that doesn't involve your stove this summer, it's worth keeping in rotation.</p>
