<p>Ordering sushi for a team lunch is a straightforward way to feed an office without everyone driving separately. Here's how to make it work smoothly.</p>
<h2>Ordering Ahead</h2>
<p>The most reliable approach is placing your order online in advance and scheduling a pickup time that fits your team's schedule, rather than calling in a large order last minute. That gives the kitchen time to prepare everything properly instead of rushing a big order.</p>
<h2>Building a Mixed Order</h2>
<p>Offices usually have mixed preferences, so it helps to order a range: a few standard rolls (California, spicy tuna), one or two <a href="/inside-raw-sushi-bars-signature-special-rolls-stockton.html">signature special rolls</a> for the more adventurous eaters, and a cooked option like teriyaki or tempura for anyone who'd rather skip raw fish. Our <a href="/never-had-sushi-cooked-options-at-raw-sushi-bar-stockton.html">cooked-options guide</a> is worth sharing with the team beforehand.</p>
<h2>Handling the Bill</h2>
<p>If one person is expensing the order, a single online order keeps it as one transaction. If the team is splitting costs individually, it's worth deciding that ahead of time — check splitting in-house is available for parties of four or fewer, but a large takeout order works differently since it's picked up rather than served at a table.</p>
<h2>Timing the Pickup</h2>
<p>Building in a buffer before your scheduled pickup time helps avoid a rushed walk back to the office with food that's already been sitting.</p>
